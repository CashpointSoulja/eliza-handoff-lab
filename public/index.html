An independent study of the care-enquiry handoff

A family calls a care home at 7pm. Who picks up, and who calls back?

Lottie's public Eliza role posting says a third of enquiries to care providers go unanswered[1]. This prototype explores one part of that problem, the handoff from AI to a person. It uses a synthetic provider, 15 labelled synthetic enquiries, three policy versions and a release gate.

Answered on the provider's behalf

The posting describes Eliza, inside Found, answering calls and live chats from families on the provider's behalf, any time of day[1].

Promises checked against the rota

In this prototype, v2 only promises a callback from someone on the synthetic rota at that hour. v1 does not check.

Safeguarding is a hard gate

A candidate policy that misses one labelled safeguarding case is blocked, however fast it is.

One enquiry, two sides

The family is trying to find care. The provider's team is trying not to lose them. The prototype models the moment in between.

  1. 1
    Context, not modelled

    A family searches for care

    Lottie says families use its marketplace to find care homes and home care[1]. Its site shows listings with confirmed availability and detailed pricing[2].

  2. 2
    In the Playground

    The enquiry reaches the provider

    A voice call or live chat comes in on a tracked synthetic number, such as a marketplace profile, the provider's website or a hospital discharge leaflet, often out of hours. The posting names call tracking and attribution as part of the product[1].

  3. 3
    In Provider setup

    The AI follows the provider's rules

    Each synthetic provider sets what may be said about fees and rooms, who covers out of hours, and who the safeguarding lead is. The posting describes their phone trees, their out-of-hours cover, their weekend rotas, their CRM[1].

  4. 4
    In the Playground

    A person takes over, with the context

    Safeguarding, clinical and urgent enquiries go to a named person who is actually on the rota. That person receives a handoff packet, so the family doesn't have to repeat themselves. The posting frames the goal as neither side drops the family in between[1].

  5. 5
    In Evals

    Every change is gated before release

    This prototype builds the items the posting lists: labelled call sets, automated scoring, regression tests before every release, and a human review loop[1]. Here that is a synthetic set and a reviewer queue that stays in your browser.

Three policies, one story

The same 15 conversations, run through three versions of the handoff policy.

Baseline

v1, contain-first

It has the highest containment (60% on the synthetic set), but it quotes internal prices, promises rooms, transfers to empty rotas and misses safeguarding.

Passes gate

v2, handoff-aware

It checks the rota and the provider's rules, and routes safeguarding and clinical questions to named people. It passes every labelled case.

Blocked

v3, fast path

Its declared latency budget is 700 ms lower than v2's, but its lighter safeguarding check misses the indirect disclosure in S04. The posting asks when a faster model is worth a slightly worse answer[1]. On a safety-critical path, the gate says it is not.
